# Shapes

Four shapes cover everything in `drafts/` so far. Pick one deliberately and tell him
which you picked — mixing two is how a post ends up with no spine.

## 1. Origin story

_"How Well Bookmarked started", "How I started using Home Assistant"._ The house
style. No headings, ~450–700 words.

1. **The shout** — a number, a fragment, a scene. One or two lines.
2. **The deflate** — walk the shout back to the real, smaller number.
3. **The itch** — the specific, named, slightly stupid problem. Concrete example.
4. **What already existed and why it failed** — brief, no vendor-bashing, land on
   the one thing missing.
5. **What I did** — the simple idea, in one sentence, no architecture.
6. **The ugly part** — the fix created a new problem, or the idea had a limit. This
   beat is mandatory.
7. **Why it stuck** — usually because he uses the thing daily. This is where the
   "I normally quit after two weeks" line lives, if it's going to live anywhere.
8. **Hand it over** — invitation to the reader, small ask, one-line sign-off.

## 2. Field list

_"5 uses of Home Assistant"._ Real content, list-shaped — not listicle bait.

- Two or three short paragraphs of framing first: why this list exists, what it cost
  to learn. Never open on item one.
- `##` per item, plain-label headings.
- Each item: what it does → why he built it → what it cost or what broke. Three
  sentences minimum, and **deliberately uneven** — one item gets six sentences and
  a price list, another gets two. Even sections read as filler.
- Numbers in the title must match the number of sections. He'll notice.
- Close by naming the one item a reader should start with, and why.

## 3. Comparison / reconsideration

_"Mac vs Windows"._ The dangerous one — this is where posts turn into specs.

- **Declare the bias in the first paragraph.** "My first computer ran Windows XP and
  I stayed there until I graduated." The reader needs to know whose thumb is on the
  scale.
- **A timeline, not a table.** What he used, when, and what pushed him off it.
- `##` per criterion, and only criteria he has actually lived — the tooling, the node
  processes that run slower on the XPS, the 3D dev that Mac couldn't do. Never a
  criterion he'd have to research.
- **No benchmark theatre.** He says it himself: "I am not here to report a benchmark
  comparison." Lived comparisons only, and say that's what they are.
- **Pick a side and pay for it.** The verdict names what it costs him. False balance
  is the failure mode.

## 4. Postmortem

_Not in `drafts/` yet — propose it when a topic is really about something he
dropped._ The abandoned side project, the setup he tore out, the tool he stopped
using. Honest, unheroic, ~400 words: what he wanted → what he built → the exact week
it stopped being fun → what he'd keep. No redemption arc, no "lessons learned"
listicle at the end. This is the one shape where the quitting trait is the subject
rather than a joke, so drop the joke entirely — it can't be both.

## Length

400–800 words. Under 300 and it's a note, not a post. Over 1,000 and it's two posts —
say which two, and propose shipping the better one first. Then actually stop; a
second post proposed is a second post postponed unless it has a slug and a file.
