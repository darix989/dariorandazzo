# Voice

The reference sample is [how-well-bookmarked-started.md](../../../../src/content/blog/how-well-bookmarked-started.md).
Read it before drafting. When a rule here and that post disagree, the post wins.

## The fingerprint

**Short paragraphs.** One to three sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs are normal
and do the emphasis work that bold would do in worse writing. Nothing over four
sentences.

**Open with a fragment, a number, or a shout — never a thesis.** `IT IS OVER 9000!`
Then deflate it in the next paragraph: "Okay, they weren't actually over 9000. They
were about 3,000. Still a lot." Setup, puncture, move on. The reader should be a
sentence into the story before they realize the article started.

**Concrete anchors, always.** Not "a bookmark I needed often" but "Amazon's Contact
Us page". Not "cheap sensors" but "Aqara door contact sensors, under $20 CAD each".
Real names, real prices, real tags in backticks (`help`, `eshop`). The specificity
_is_ the credibility — which is exactly why inventing one is unforgivable.

**Questions in the reader's head, not the author's.** "The problem with that: where
the hell did I put it? What was it called? Which folder was it in?" Three quick ones
in a row, then answer none of them directly.

**Mild profanity, rationed.** "where the hell" — about one per post, and only where
the frustration is real.

**Second person arrives late.** The post is "I" until near the end, then turns to
"you" to hand something over: "If your bookmark bar looks like mine used to, give it
a few days."

**Admit the ugly part.** Every post has a moment where the thing didn't work, or the
fix created a new problem: "Spoiler: if you get too many tags, you forget those too."
"Once the setup felt pretty good, another problem showed up: duplicate bookmarks."
This is the most characteristic move in the whole voice. If a draft has no such
moment, the draft is hiding something — go find it.

**Close with a nudge and a sign-off.** A small ask ("consider leaving a rating"),
then a short line: "Happy bookmarking!" Warm, one line, no recap.

**Headings**: a short story post uses none. List and comparison posts use `##` only,
in lowercase-ish plain words — a label, not a sentence, and never a question.

**No emoji. No bold sprinkled for emphasis. No bullet list unless the content is
genuinely a list** (parts, prices, steps).

## Italian-English cleanups

He's an Italian speaker writing in English, and the scratch drafts carry a consistent
set of L1 patterns. Fix these silently — they're typos, not voice.

| In the drafts                                | Write                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| "I use to forget", "I use to postpone"       | "I forget", "I postpone" (habitual = present simple) |
| "I sticked to Windows"                       | "I stuck with Windows"                               |
| "I felt for the Apple's charm"               | "I fell for Apple's charm"                           |
| "it lacked of customization"                 | "it lacked customization"                            |
| "for almost an year"                         | "for almost a year"                                  |
| "AliExpress was going to be my best friends" | "…my best friend"                                    |
| "the pieces I need" (past narrative)         | "the pieces I needed"                                |
| "one of my most satisfying hobby"            | "one of the most satisfying hobbies I've had"        |
| "way way slower"                             | keep — that one's voice, not error                   |

Also: he writes "Then, I can't remember why, I felt…" — the comma-spliced aside is
his rhythm. Keep the asides, fix the grammar around them.

Currency stays as written, with the code: `$40 CAD`, not `$40` or `40 dollars`.

## What to strip on sight

**AI tells** — if any of these survive into a draft, the post reads as generated:

- "In today's fast-paced…", "In the world of…", "Let's dive in", "Let's explore"
- "delve", "leverage", "seamlessly", "robust", "game-changer", "unlock", "elevate"
- "It's not just X — it's Y" and every other antithesis-as-punchline
- "In conclusion", "At the end of the day", "The bottom line"
- Rule-of-three lists everywhere ("faster, cleaner, and more maintainable")
- A heading for every 80 words
- Hedging stacks: "it might arguably be somewhat useful in certain cases"
- Symmetrical paragraphs, all the same length, all the same shape
- Announcing the structure: "First, I'll cover X. Then Y."
- "As a developer, I…" / "As someone with OCD, I…"

**Résumé voice** — "I architected a solution leveraging". He fixes streaming apps on
TVs for a living and says exactly that. Match it.

**Tutorial voice** — this is a changelog, not documentation. "You should configure…"
becomes "I configured…, and here's what broke". If a post genuinely needs steps,
keep them to a short list and stay in the first person around it.

## Sentence-level test

Read the draft aloud in your head. If three sentences in a row have the same length
and the same subject-verb-object shape, break one. If a paragraph could open a
LinkedIn post, delete it.
